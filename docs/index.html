Sources

  • Forbes NBA team valuations, 2016 to 2026 editions, from archived copies of the data behind Forbes' list page on the Wayback Machine (one link per row in data/nba_valuations.csv).
  • BasketNews EuroLeague budget research: 2021-22, 2024-25, 2025-26.
  • EuroLeague standings from the league's open API (api-live.euroleague.net), regular season only.
  • JB Capital valuation: EuroLeague press release, March 2026.
  • French league: LNB/DNCCG budgets 2025-26 and DNCCG accounts summary 2024-25.
  • Sportico and CNBC values as republished by Bleacher Report and NBC Chicago.
  • NBA deal prices: ESPN, Sports Illustrated, Hoops Rumors, Axios; one link per deal in data/manual/transactions.csv.
  • Share prices and exchange rates: Yahoo Finance, through the yfinance library.
  • US inflation: FRED series CPIAUCSL (used for one number in the note pack only).

Caveats

  • The listed index only holds clubs that are still listed today. Clubs that delisted, like AS Roma in 2022, are missing from the whole history, so the index probably flatters what an investor would actually have earned.
  • Start dates change the answer a lot. From 2004 the index grows 4.3% a year, from 2012 12.2%; over the same months (2012-09 to 2026-11) the two give the same result, so the gap comes from the start date, not from which clubs are in.
  • Club prices, the S&P 500 and IEUR include dividends. STOXX Europe 600 is price only and so understates that benchmark. It is only there to cover the years before IEUR started (2014).
  • EuroLeague budgets are BasketNews estimates with a stated margin of error, and I keep the ranges they report. Payroll and total budget are never mixed in one series. The 2025-26 payroll counts players only, earlier seasons include coaches.
  • The 2021-22 standings leave out the three Russian clubs expelled during the season, and Baskonia published no figures for 2021-22.
  • Valuations are estimates, not prices, and the publishers disagree. Sportico's Oct 2026 values are on average 5.2% above Forbes for the 4 teams whose figures I could find published; CNBC's Feb 2026 values are 4.1% above Forbes' Oct 2025 list for 15 teams. Neither full list is available outside the publishers' own sites.
  • A deal valuation and a Forbes value aren't the same measure: deals can include arenas or other businesses, and some are paid in stages (the Trail Blazers row uses the first tranche).
  • French budgets are what clubs filed with the league each September, not audited accounts. The audited club accounts sit in a separate download; the loss figures here come from the league's own summary of them: a €34.0M operating loss for the division in 2024-25, €32.2M of it from the three EuroLeague clubs.
  • Two tickers changed in 2026. MSG Sports (Knicks) trades as MSGK after spinning off the Rangers, and Eagle Football Group (Lyon) is now OL Groupe (OLG). Both keep their full price history.
  • Index membership is a judgement call: Turkish clubs are left out (currency swings and speculative trading), TKO's pre-2023 WWE history is dropped, and the US companies are kept separate from the European index. methodology.md explains each choice and the alternative.
  • The Swiss SB League is left out because its clubs don't publish financial figures.
Python, pandas and plotly. Share prices refresh on the 3rd of each month through GitHub Actions; the yearly data is updated by hand.
